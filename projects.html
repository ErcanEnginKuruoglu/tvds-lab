Heavy-Tailed Statistics

α-Stable Learning & Non-Gaussian Processes

Researchers: Bo Shu · Jipeng Li

Gaussian assumptions can fail when data, noise, or learned parameters are heavy-tailed, asymmetric, and impulsive. We develop α-stable and other non-Gaussian models for stochastic processes and machine learning — from robust Bayesian inference under heavy-tailed noise to understanding α-stable structure in neural-network weights and exploiting it for more robust, efficient learning.

Representation Learning

Structured & Efficient Representation Learning

Researcher: Chengfeng Wu

Better learning does not always require more complex optimization. We design representations that expose the structure a model actually needs — separating task-relevant semantics from nuisance information in multi-task learning, and replacing expensive attention with efficient temporal aggregation on dynamic graphs. The goal is models that share information more effectively, generalize under distribution shift, and scale to rapidly evolving data.

Brain Network Learning

LOSC — Learning Optimal Spectral Clustering

Functional brain networks exhibit a small-world topology, where brain regions form functional clusters whose abnormalities are strongly associated with disease. LOSC is a framework that integrates brain-network generation, clustering, and classification with a graph-theory-grounded Rayleigh Quotient Loss to preserve these small-world properties. By leveraging the resulting functional clusters, LOSC delivers consistent accuracy gains of 2.0%, 3.6%, and 2.6% on the ABIDE, ADHD-200, and HCP datasets over state-of-the-art models — and the discovered clusters align with known neuropathology, surfacing new functional-community biomarkers.

Fig. 1 — The LOSC pipeline: brain connectivity is learned in a spatio–spectral embedding space, partitioned into functionally synchronized clusters, then classified using intra- and inter-cluster relations.

Brain Network Learning

HB-GIB — Hierarchical Brain Graph Information Bottleneck

Functional brain networks are often modeled as dense, fully connected graphs, which limits both scalability and interpretability. HB-GIB identifies community-level graph information bottlenecks to preserve biologically meaningful structure, then applies a masked attention mechanism to capture cross-community interactions guided by the learned sparse topology. Treating the brain as a complex system, it incorporates Tsallis entropy and a self-supervised pre-training strategy for robustness under limited imaging. Across four neuroimaging datasets, HB-GIB reaches state-of-the-art disorder classification while enabling node- and community-level interpretation.

Fig. 2 — HB-GIB preserves the brain's hierarchical community structure while modeling cross-community interactions through masked attention.
