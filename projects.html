Projects

A closer look at the flagship efforts of the Time-Varying Data Science Group — each led by our students and postdocs, with selected peer-reviewed outputs. For the complete record, see the publications.

Graph Signal Processing

Time-Varying Graph Signals

Real networks — sensor grids, markets, brains — carry signals on nodes and edges whose statistics drift over time. We build online estimators and adaptive graph filters that track these signals as they evolve, and pair them with large language models for forecasting on time-varying graphs.

Researchers: Yi Yan · Dayu Qin

Selected outputs
Adaptive Joint Estimation of Temporal Vertex and Edge Signals IEEE Trans. Signal & Information Processing over Networks, vol. 11, 2025 · View paper → LLM-based Online Prediction of Time-varying Graph Signals AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 2025 · View paper →

Bayesian Model Selection

Efficient Deep Learning

Big networks are wasteful. Using reversible-jump MCMC and simulated annealing, we let a Bayesian search decide how large a model needs to be — pruning weights and sizing architectures automatically, so systems stay parameter-efficient without hand-tuning or accuracy loss.

Researchers: Peter Marsh

Selected outputs
Optimized Parameter-Efficient Deep Learning Systems via Reversible Jump Simulated Annealing IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Signal Processing, vol. 18, 2024 · View paper →

Topological Data Analysis

Topological Cancer Genomics

Genes act in networks, not in isolation. We bring graph and topological signal processing to gene co-expression networks — using tools like HodgeRank to prioritise cancer-driver genes and expose stage-, subtype-, and pathway-specific structure that single-gene expression misses.

Researchers: Katie · Yutong Jiang

Selected outputs
Prioritizing Cancer Genes Using HodgeRank Reveals Critical Pathways in Cancer Progression IEEE Conference on Artificial Intelligence (CAI), 2025 · View paper → Graph Frequency Features of Cancer Gene Co-Expression Networks Preprint, 2023 (arXiv:2311.06747) · View paper →

Heavy-Tailed Statistics

Heavy Tails & Financial Risk

Market crashes are not outliers to be discarded — they are the events that matter most. We model them with α-stable graphical models and graph learning that detect and predict systemic risk, treating heavy tails as a first-class part of the theory.

Researchers: Dayu Qin · Taurai Muvunza

Selected outputs
Graph Learning Based Financial Market Crash Identification and Prediction IEEE Conference on Artificial Intelligence (CAI), 2024 · View paper → Stable Probabilistic Graphical Models for Systemic Risk Estimation IEEE Conference on Artificial Intelligence (CAI), 2024 · View paper →

Bayesian Deep Learning

Bayesian Uncertainty

A prediction without a confidence interval can be dangerous. We design neural networks that know what they don't know — through noise-injection training and minimax priors — turning point estimates into calibrated uncertainty that widens where data is scarce.

Researchers: Xueqiong Yuan · Junping Hong

Selected outputs
